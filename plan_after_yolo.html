From Benchmark to Board

The YOLO comparison is settled and the win is real. But 16 real boards show the model is not usable at its default settings — and the reason is not accuracy. This is what was measured, what was fixed today, and what remains.

What is already settled

Both models were scored on the same 432 test images, through one metric implementation, with 1,000 paired bootstrap draws. The capture was verified to reproduce the published pipeline number to zero drift before any interval was computed.

Two corrections travel with this number. The lead is +0.048, not the +0.10 in the existing report — that figure compared a mid-training V6.2 against a YOLO stopped at epoch 23 of 300. And mAP50 is a statistical tie: its interval includes zero, so at the detection threshold the two models are indistinguishable.

That tie is the most useful thing in the table. V6.2’s entire advantage is mask geometry, not detection — exactly what the architecture argument predicted, since low-rank prototype masks and box-cropped masks both bite at high IoU and vanish at IoU 0.50.

And +0.048 may itself be generous. The Tier 4 repair below found that the ground truth these numbers were measured against was damaged in a way that favoured V6.2: Ultralytics paints smallest-last, so YOLO always trained on the correct nesting, while a YOLO correctly predicting a hole was scored against a solid mask. The re-run is parked until V6.2 is retrained on repaired targets — scoring the current weights against repaired ground truth would charge V6.2 for a defect the repair just fixed. This is the second published figure this work has revised downward today; both revisions came from measuring something that had been assumed.

One caveat runs the other way, and belongs here for exactly the same reason. A controlled rotation sweep — two boards, seven angles, scale held fixed — found V6.2’s median detection confidence varies by 0.044 across orientations while YOLO’s varies by 0.886 and drops below 0.5 on four of fourteen angle/board pairs. YOLO trains at degrees=0.0 and never sees a rotated pad; V6.2’s cycle sweeps 45 rotations. Step 7 scored whatever orientation the test split happens to carry.

Held at the strength the evidence supports: that is confidence, not accuracy, on two unlabelled boards, and YOLO’s collapses are erratic rather than a clean penalty at 45°. Step 7 ranks detections from conf=0.001 and mask AP is rank-based, so a uniform confidence drop would barely move it — only a selective one would. This is a reason to measure the test split at several rotations, not a correction to apply to +0.048.

Failure one — phantom duplicates

54% of all detections sat in the 0.2–0.4 band, and they were duplicates: a spurious low-confidence Rectangle laid on top of pads already found correctly. The class mix read 80.4% Rectangle against 39.7% in the training data. CENTER_CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD = 0.10 was admitting weak spurious centre peaks as instances. On a labelled benchmark that costs almost nothing, because mask AP is rank-aware and they sort to the bottom — on a real board there is no ranking to hide behind, and every one is drawn on the picture.

Failure two — large objects shatter

On the zoomed-in images a single pad becomes seven detections with the mask carved into vertical strips. That is offset-based grouping failing: every pixel predicts a vector to its object’s centre, and for a 130 px object those vectors are far longer than the head was ever trained to emit. Thresholds do not touch this one.

One further measurement: these 3:2 sources letterbox to 512×341 inside a 512² frame, so 33.4% of every forward pass is grey padding. That is a third of the compute spent on nothing, and it shrinks every object by 1.5× before the model sees it.

Tier 0

Ship the deployment operating pointApplied

No retraining

A better threshold set was already found on 5 September and survived a held-out half of validation, but nothing outside reevaluate.py ever applied it — every prediction run still used values inherited from Model V5. deployment.py now carries that profile, and both prediction entry points use it.

A confidence floor of 0.50 follows it, because centre-less fallback recoveries score FALLBACK_INSTANCE_SCORE = 0.05 and survive thresholds that act on centre evidence they do not have. Together: 1,074 detections down to 209, with all 194 confident detections untouched, and both low bands empty for the first time.

This is not simply the better configuration. On the labelled test split these thresholds score marginally worse — mAP50-95 0.7711 → 0.7694 — because they buy precision with recall. Same model, genuinely different right answer for the two jobs. The benchmark defaults in model_v6_2.py are unchanged and DEPLOYMENT_MIN_CONFIDENCE is 0.0 there, so every published number stays reproducible.

Those 16 boards are a worst case, not a typical one, and the distinction matters before this number is quoted. They were supplied as boards the model was failing on, so a 54% duplicate rate is what a curated failure set looks like. On 40 images sampled from a different lot, V6.2 puts 2.7% of detections below 0.2 and 73.5% above 0.8, and the deployment profile removes almost nothing — 536 detections become 534, against 1,074 → 209 on the failure set.

That does not argue against shipping it: the profile costs nothing when there is nothing to remove, and it rescues the boards where the model is unusable. It does mean the honest claim is “fixes the bad boards” rather than “removes 81% of false detections”, which was one set generalised into a headline.

Tier 1

Widen the training scale range

~3 days · retrain

Change EXTRA_ZOOM_RANGE from (1.06, 1.12) to roughly (1.0, 3.0), keeping the zoom-out ladder. Training then spans about 0.5×–3× instead of 0.5×–1.7×.

This is the highest-value training change available because it hits three problems at once: the fragmentation above, the known train→test scale shift where test objects are 0.48–0.76× training size, and the circle class that collapses at 16 px. It is a small change carried by one retrain.

Tier 2

Replace the grouping, restore the resolution

~3 days · same retrain

Distance-transform watershed. Swap the two-channel offset vector field for one channel of per-instance-normalised inner distance. That field is scale-free by construction — it cannot fail the way offsets fail on a 130 px object. It also frees 22.6% of all MACs and deletes the fallback path that mints the 0.05-score fragments.

Full-resolution decoders. The opposite end of the same problem: a 16 px annulus has a ring under two pixels wide at the current 256² head resolution, which is where circle AP50-95 0.5630 comes from. The grouping change frees the compute the resolution change needs, so the two are one job and not two.

Two independent confirmations that these are one job. The grouping failure reproduces on a different lot: on an image carrying a long diagonal copper strip, V6.2 shatters it into pieces with ragged edges and an interior hole while YOLO — which has no offset field — returns it as a single clean mask. That is the same defect the 16 failure boards show, on a different set and against a model that cannot exhibit it.

And the replacement grouping has now been built and tested against ground truth ahead of the retrain. It reproduces every synthetic scene at IoU 1.000 — including a 130 px object beside a 16 px one, the pairing the offset field could not span — and survives realistic prediction noise on compact objects. It does not survive it on a thin annulus, because at 256² that ring’s core is a sub-pixel-wide dotted line and no threshold recovers information the resolution never carried. Replacing the grouping will not fix circle on its own — it needs the resolution alongside it. That is now measured rather than argued from the memory budget.

Tier 3

Stop letterboxing to a square

Efficiency & precision

A 3:2 source in a square frame wastes 33.4% of every forward pass. A 3:2 input tensor — 768×512 — or tiling reclaims that compute and makes every object 1.5× larger in the model’s frame, which improves precision on exactly the small objects that dominate the remaining loss. It is the rare change that makes the model both faster and more accurate. Inference is currently 5.4 s/image, most of it CPU-side instance grouping; Tier 2 and Tier 3 attack both halves.

Tier 4

The data ceilingApplied

Repaired · 1.6 min

The expected cause was duplicate polygons. Measuring first said otherwise: on test, nesting is 98.3% of the shared pixels and there are no duplicates at all. Every one of the 36 nested pairs is the same convention — a small Rectangle inside a Rectangle_concave at a 3.5–3.8% area ratio. Under file-order painting whichever polygon came last won, so some pairs ended with the concave solid and its inner Rectangle erased, and others with the concave annular; 16 instances were lost outright. The same physical configuration was labelled two ways depending on serialisation order, and that inconsistency is the real damage, not the 16 erasures alone.

rasterize_instances now resolves contested pixels explicitly instead of letting file order decide. Verified across the full test split: 33,488 polygons in, 33,488 instances out, zero destroyed, and zero order-dependent images under a reversed-order control — which tests the defect rather than its symptom. Repaired arrays live in a new directory so the arrays behind the published figure stay byte-intact.

A prediction, stated before the retrain so it can be falsified. Rectangle_concave is the rarest class — 680 training polygons, 0.47% — and the audit calls it the structural limit needing more labels. But part of that weakness was never a data-volume problem: those 680 polygons were rasterised inconsistently, so the model was asked to learn a contradiction on precisely the class with the least evidence to resolve it. Consistent targets are worth disproportionately more at 680 polygons than at 76,612. If the retrain moves one class further than the rest, it should be this one.

Bounded, so the prediction can actually fail: train carries 64 nested pairs, which is 9.4% of the 680. Real, but not most of the class — so the claim is that Rectangle_concave moves further than the other three, not that it is transformed. If it moves by less than the noise between checkpoints, the claim is simply wrong.

What the repair cannot fix is volume. Large and rare remains the hard combination, and no architecture change substitutes for labels — it is now a smaller problem than the audit implied, not an absent one.

Order of work

  1. Tier 0 — done. Free, immediate, and verified on the 16 boards.
  2. Tier 4 — done. Repaired targets are prepared and waiting; the retrain no longer trains on a contradiction.
  3. Tiers 1, 2 and 3 as a single retrain. They need the same ~3-day run and their benefits compound: a wider scale range needs grouping that survives it, and both want the resolution Tier 3 hands back.
  4. Re-run step 7 afterwards, not before. The harness exists and takes about 90 minutes. Running it on repaired ground truth with the current weights would measure the old defect, not the model.
  5. Add a rotation sweep to that re-run. Score the labelled test split at several angles rather than only the orientation it happens to carry. That turns the two-board confidence finding into an interval, and it costs one extra pass of a harness that already exists.

The YOLO result holds — an interval that excludes zero, ahead in every one of 1,000 draws. What has changed today is confidence in its size: +0.10 became +0.048, and +0.048 now carries a known bias in V6.2’s favour that only the retrain can settle. The direction of the claim has survived every measurement; its magnitude has shrunk under each one.
